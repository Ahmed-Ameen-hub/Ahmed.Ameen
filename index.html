About

Short bio

I work at the intersection of Islamic/Ottoman architectural history, epigraphy, and evidence-based heritage studies. My recent work also engages heritage science through multi-analytical study of manuscript materials.

Professor, History and Islamic Civilization, University of Sharjah (UAE)

My research documents and interprets Ottoman monuments and their inscriptions in Greece and the Balkans, and extends to Ayyubid madrasas in Cairo, Qur’anic epigraphy in Quanzhou, the UAE’s Islamic heritage, and the material analysis of Islamic–African manuscripts. I hold a PhD in Islamic Archaeology (University of Athens, 2010) and have served as Editor-in-Chief of SHEDET, a Scopus-indexed journal. My book Islamic Architecture in Greece: Mosques (Bibliotheca Alexandrina, 2017) catalogues the mosque heritage of a country with roughly 300 Islamic antiquities.

Why cite this work?

If your own research touches any of the situations below, one of these papers offers a ready evidence base, method or open question you can build on. Each publication below has a “Why cite this?” note.

  • Are you describing Ottoman mosques, markets or madrasas in Greece or the Balkans? Start from the documented corpus, typologies and patronage studies here.
  • Are you reading a foundation or dedicatory inscription? See how inscriptions were used to re-date, re-attribute and reinterpret monuments.
  • Does colour affect how an inscription is seen and read? The Didymóteichon study offers a case and a framework for visual impact.
  • Are you mapping waqf institutions or madrasas in Ayyubid Cairo? Use the site-based analytical approach and the documentary evidence.
  • Are you studying amulets, talismans or paper manuscripts? See a multi-technique workflow (imaging, FTIR, Raman, LIBS, LIF) applied to Islamic–African collections.
  • Are you using travellers’ accounts such as Evliyâ Çelebi to study a lost or altered monument? See how they are tested against surviving buildings.
  • Are you working with Islamic coins, chronicles or manuscript certificates as historical sources? See the source-critical approaches applied to them.
  • Do you need a precedent for linking text, patron and architecture? Several papers show how epigraphy, waqf and building evidence reinforce one another.

Research themes

1) Architecture

  • Mosque architecture (UAE, Ottoman world)
  • Urban/commercial buildings (bedestens) & built heritage
  • Comparative architectural history and typologies

2) Epigraphy & inscriptions

  • Ottoman inscriptions in Greece/Balkans
  • Color, visibility, and the visual identity of inscriptions
  • Multilingual inscriptions and public communication

3) Waqf & madrasas

  • Independent madrasas in Cairo (Ayyubid period)
  • Institutional/urban history and educational geography
  • Foundations, patronage, and documentary/inscriptional evidence

4) Heritage science (manuscripts)

  • Material and multi-analytical study of paper manuscripts
  • Conservation science and evidence-based preservation
  • Museum collections and Islamic–African manuscript heritage
